# Peer review and critical assessment

Related: `bai-bao-khoa-hoc` (responding to reviews), `bao-cao-ket-qua` (checking results and statistics), `ai-trong-hoc-thuat` (AI and confidentiality), `van-phong-hoc-thuat` (tone).

## 1. Before accepting (COPE Ethical Guidelines for Peer Reviewers)
- Accept only work within your expertise and only if you can meet the deadline.
- Declare competing interests (collaborator, competitor, same institution, personal relationship); decline or ask the editor if unsure. If one appears mid-review, tell the editor at once.
- Keep the manuscript confidential during and after review; do not share it, use its ideas or data for your own work, or contact the authors.
- Do not upload it to generative AI tools.
- Be objective and constructive; no personal remarks.

## 2. Reading order
1. Read title, abstract, introduction, conclusion: what is claimed?
2. Read the whole paper once without notes.
3. Second pass with notes, section by section, against §3.
4. Check key references and that the cited work says what the paper says it does (spot-check).

## 3. What to evaluate
| Aspect | Questions |
|---|---|
| Question and relevance | Is the problem clear and important for this venue/field? Fits the scope? |
| Novelty and positioning | What is new relative to cited prior work? Are key related works missing? |
| Method | Appropriate for the question? Described enough to reproduce? Assumptions stated? |
| Data | Source, size, quality, leakage, ethics/consent, availability |
| Evaluation | Baselines fair and strong? Metrics suitable? Statistics (n, variance, tests)? Ablations? |
| Claims vs evidence | Does every conclusion follow from the results shown? Overclaims, generalisation beyond data? |
| Limitations | Acknowledged honestly? Threats to validity discussed? |
| Presentation | Structure, clarity, figures/tables readable and self-contained, language |
| References | Adequate, current, correctly cited, no suspicious or irrelevant self-citation |
| Ethics and integrity | Plagiarism, duplicate publication, image manipulation, undisclosed AI use, human-subject approval |

For theses add: fit between title, objectives, methods and conclusions; whether objectives were met; the candidate's own contribution; compliance with formatting rules (`trinh-bay-van-ban-hoc-thuat`); practical applicability (đề án).

## 4. Report structure (journal/conference)
1. **Summary** (3–5 sentences, neutral): what the paper does and claims. Shows the authors you understood it.
2. **Overall assessment:** main strengths and main weaknesses in 2–4 sentences.
3. **Major issues** (numbered): problems that affect validity or conclusions. For each: what, where (section/figure), why it matters, what would fix it (analysis, experiment, rewording claims).
4. **Minor issues** (numbered): clarity, missing details, typos, figure readability, reference formatting.
5. **Recommendation** (often in a separate field): accept / minor revision / major revision / reject (some venues: reject and resubmit; conferences use scores and confidence).
6. **Confidential comments to the editor** (separate field): concerns about ethics, conflicts, your confidence.

Write so issues can be answered point by point (R1.1, R1.2… in the response letter).

## 5. Tone
- Address the work, not the authors: "The evaluation does not include…" not "The authors failed to…".
- Be specific and actionable: cite section, table, line; suggest the analysis or text needed.
- Separate necessary changes from suggestions ("optional: …").
- Do not demand citations of your own work unless genuinely essential.
- Do not rewrite the paper in your preferred style; flag only real clarity problems.
- Calibrate: do not ask for experiments disproportionate to the contribution.

## 6. Thesis review (nhận xét phản biện luận văn/đề án) — Vietnamese convention
The school form usually has these headings; fill each with concrete evidence:
1. Tính cấp thiết, ý nghĩa khoa học và thực tiễn của đề tài.
2. Sự phù hợp giữa tên đề tài, mục tiêu, nội dung với chuyên ngành.
3. Tổng quan và cơ sở lý thuyết (đầy đủ, cập nhật, có đánh giá).
4. Phương pháp nghiên cứu, độ tin cậy của số liệu.
5. Kết quả đạt được, đóng góp mới của học viên.
6. Hình thức trình bày, trích dẫn, tài liệu tham khảo.
7. Hạn chế, thiếu sót cần chỉnh sửa (đánh số).
8. Câu hỏi cho học viên (2–5 câu, tập trung vào điểm chính).
9. Kết luận: đồng ý/không đồng ý cho bảo vệ; điểm (thang điểm theo quy định trường).
Write in neutral academic Vietnamese; each criticism points to a chapter/section/page.

## 7. Self-review before submission
Run §3 on your own manuscript, or ask an independent reader (a different person or model, review-only) to produce a §4-style report; then fix major issues first.
