## 2. Personal names
- **Foreign names in Vietnamese text:** keep the original Latin spelling (Shannon, Vaswani), do not transliterate phonetically. Chinese/Japanese/Korean authors: use the romanized form printed in the source (Zhang Wei, Tanaka). Russian etc.: the transliteration in the source.
- **Vietnamese names in English text:** keep diacritics if the venue/template supports Unicode (Nguyễn Văn An), else drop them consistently (Nguyen Van An). Order: use the same order as the person's own prior publications/ORCID; common forms are "Nguyen Van An" or "An Van Nguyen". Never mix forms for the same author.
- **Citations of Vietnamese authors in English papers:** use the name as printed on the cited work.
- Titles (TS., PGS.) are not part of names in reference lists.

## 3. Place names, organisations
- Countries and well-known places in Vietnamese text use the established Vietnamese name: Hoa Kỳ (Mỹ), Anh, Pháp, Đức, Nhật Bản, Hàn Quốc, Trung Quốc, Bắc Kinh, Luân Đôn (or London; pick one), Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh. Less common places: original Latin spelling.
- International organisations: Vietnamese name if established (Liên Hợp Quốc, Tổ chức Y tế Thế giới (WHO), Ngân hàng Thế giới), with the English acronym at first mention.
- Companies, products, software, datasets, methods: original name, never translated (Google, PostgreSQL, ImageNet, Transformer, BERT).
- Vietnamese place names in English: with or without diacritics consistently; Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi (standard English), Da Nang.

## 4. Titles of theses and papers
**Vietnamese → English:**
- Noun phrase, not a sentence; drop "Nghiên cứu…", "Tìm hiểu…", "Xây dựng…" unless the work is literally a build: "Xây dựng hệ thống X cho Y" → *A X system for Y* / *Building a X system for Y* (both acceptable; prefer what the venue uses).
- Avoid "A study on", "Research on", "Some solutions to".
- Capitalization: title case for English titles (unless the venue uses sentence case).
- Keep it specific: method + task + data/context: *Hybrid retrieval for Vietnamese legal question answering*.

**English → Vietnamese:**
- Vietnamese title in sentence case (only first letter and proper names capitalized).
- Translate the meaning, keep method/model names in English: *Attention Is All You Need* → in Vietnamese text cite the original title; do not translate titles of cited works in reference lists.

## 5. Abstracts (Vietnamese ↔ English)
- Translate the finished Vietnamese abstract, not a draft; then read the English alone and rewrite sentences that sound translated.
- Structure (both languages): context/problem → objective → method → main results with numbers → conclusion/significance. 150–300 words unless specified.
- Tenses in English: present for problem and contribution ("This thesis proposes…"), past for what was done and measured ("We collected…", "The system achieved…").
- Keywords: 3–6, lowercase except proper nouns, separated by commas or semicolons per template; English keywords are translations of the Vietnamese keywords, same order.
- Numbers: switch decimal comma ↔ point, thousand separators.
- Do not add claims to the English version that are not in the Vietnamese one (and vice versa).
